Elena, who loved solving puzzles, decided to investigate. She marked the tip of the shadow every hour and recorded its position. Over several days, she discovered that the shadow didn’t just move—it danced in a perfect spiral pattern, always returning to the same spot at noon. Intrigued, she asked her grandfather, the village’s oldest storyteller, about it.

“Ah,” he said with a twinkle in his eye, “that’s not magic, Elena. It’s the Earth’s tilt and orbit. The sun doesn’t move across the sky in a straight line—it follows a path called the ecliptic. And because the Earth spins on a slanted axis, shadows trace complex patterns throughout the year.”

Elena’s eyes widened. “So the world is more complicated than it looks?”

“Exactly,” her grandfather replied. “And that’s where math comes in. Math helps us understand the hidden rules behind nature’s dance.”
