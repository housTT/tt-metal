Elena was puzzled. She had always been told that shadows move because the sun moves across the sky. But today, the shadow seemed to race ahead, as if it had a mind of its own. She decided to investigate. Grabbing her notebook and a stick, she began to measure the length of the shadow every hour, from dawn until dusk.

As the day progressed, she noticed something even stranger. The shadow didn’t just move—it seemed to change shape. At noon, it was short and sharp. By late afternoon, it stretched long and thin, almost like a snake. And when the sun began to set, the shadow seemed to disappear entirely, only to reappear on the other side of the tree, as if the tree had two shadows.

Elena’s curiosity grew. She asked her grandfather, who was known for his wisdom and knowledge of the stars. “Grandpa,” she said, “why does the shadow move so fast? And why does it change shape?”
